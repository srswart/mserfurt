# TD-018 — Thin, context-appropriate connections: owner feedback

Status: **design redirection; no new geometry or execution approval**.

The owner reviewed the connected nm nominal sample and found that construction
unnatural and too thick. Record this as rejection of this candidate for the
intended style, not rejection of every possible nm connection or a universal
historical claim. See the [exact owner feedback](../../shared/training/konrad_hand_recovery/stroke_gestures_v2/nm-construction-v1/owner-feedback-002.json).

Passing geometric constraints did not make this a useful construction. Do not
promote it to physical validation, merely thin its rendered line, or continue
optimizing it without a new design decision. Preserve the packet as negative
style evidence and leave its generated page/report immutable.

## Revised direction

- Connections should be context-appropriate, not forced because a collision-free
  path exists between two letters. Unconnected writing remains a valid choice.
- Target a nib-thin connecting stroke rather than the current broad low band.
  The exact width/profile still needs definition; no numerical threshold or
  pen-action exception is inferred from the phrase “nib thin”.
- Prioritize **nu and mu** as pair investigations. Use **num** subsequently to
  test composition of n→u and u→m, not as a full-word-specific geometry rule.
- The prior u→m retained-stroke clearance concern remains unresolved. Naming num
  as a desired context does not waive it; investigate u departure and m receiving
  construction before admitting that boundary.
- Retain upright canonical bodies, explicit pen-down/contact ownership and
  supported entry/exit construction. Do not infer that a thin upward motion
  requires a lift or that the old six-occurrence contract permits new geometry.

## Why the current stroke is thick

The nominal renderer sweeps a fixed0.45mm edge at physical-y-down40 degrees.
Its stroke width depends on travel direction relative to that edge. The long,
nearly horizontal transfer exposes a substantial transverse edge width, producing
the underline-like band. The correct next question is which plausible movement
and supported nib/contact state produce the desired thin connection—not whether
we can draw a cosmetically narrower line over the same path. Pressure reduction
alone is not a solution in this nominal renderer, which has no pressure input.

## Next bounded design work

1. Describe useful departure/receiving constructions for nu and mu and how each
   neighbor changes the contributing strokes. Include a separated alternative.
2. Define nib-thin in observable geometric terms relative to the accepted body
   stroke: local transverse width/profile, attachment behavior and no broad
   underline. Establish the numeric bounds and any supported pen-action changes
   prospectively, not by selecting the best-looking rendered outcome.
3. Investigate compatible attachment directions, finite-nib access and ownership;
   keep num's u→m dependency explicitly blocked until its concern is resolved.
4. Prepare an owner-reviewable proposal and bounded nominal evaluation scope
   before generating new geometry/images. Physical execution and fitting remain
   separately gated. No prior attempt or image allowance is reset.
