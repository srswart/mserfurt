## 3. F1 — prepare a reciprocal construction brief

### Protected identity and explicit editable regions

Keep standalone canonical n/u unchanged as the control. Protect n's first stem
and main arch and u's lower return and final stem initially. Propose bounded
replacement regions for n's terminal descent/departure and u's initial entry/
descent. Each region must specify canonical occurrence IDs, source intervals,
allowed displacement/envelope and the exact join back to the protected body.

Unlike the previous experiment, **T/S and their internal anchors are not presumed
immutable for new hypotheses**. Changes are not authorized everywhere: exact
new regions/ports must be named and approved in F1 before generating geometry.
No whole-letter slant correction or independent unowned bridge.

Keep current letter placement in the first shape comparison. However, explicitly
check whether the fixed gap itself forces a long, unnatural span. If it does,
report that constraint before changing the letters further. Propose a separate
spacing comparison only after review; never quietly tighten the gap to make a
shape look better or report a spacing effect as mutual shape adaptation.

### Two purposeful, distinguishable hypotheses

These are design hypotheses, not claims about historical ductus:

- **J1 — receiving-rise-led:** u has a deliberate entry ascent close to its
  incoming downstroke, followed by a crown/descent. n's terminal departure
  changes only as needed to feed that entry smoothly. Question: can the rise
  visibly belong to forming u instead of bridging across the gap?
- **J2 — shared accommodation:** n's terminal descent/departure and u's entry
  both adapt, distributing the direction change rather than concentrating it
  in a short shelf/shoulder or a long generic bridge. Question: does sharing the
  change produce a more natural pair while preserving each letter's identity?

F1 must turn these into different stroke programs and fixed derivation rules,
not two arbitrary parameter settings. If they reduce to the same construction,
collapse them; do not manufacture variation for the comparison.

For each, show the desired sequence in words and a port/ownership table before
specifying handles: n terminal descent → departure → u entry ascent → crown →
u descent → retained lower body. A return alongside an entry is pen-down unless
explicitly stated otherwise; do not interpret the owner's 'upward stroke' as lift.

### Required F1 specification fields

1. Purpose, editable/protected source spans, reciprocal contribution and expected
   visible difference from the old bridge.
2. One deterministic derivation rule, all numeric domains, source cuts and
   replacement envelopes; no free residual curves or optimizer-selected handles.
3. Directed ports, continuity/corner intent and any retrace/overlap ownership.
   Ordinary R-only overlap permission cannot be transplanted to new topology.
4. Fixed40° nib, fixed tool/pressure and one shared full/corner/full edge policy
   for F2. Precisely define the thin span independently of semantic naming.
5. Full comparison frame, admission checks, output inventory, budget and stop
   conditions. New anchors are explicit design changes, not measurement repairs.

## 5. F3 — nib behavior, isolated from path changes

The current renderer fixes orientation40° and varies only the active edge.
Nib rotation is therefore a genuine new variable, not something already learned.

On one owner-preferred F2 geometry, compare exactly two states:

- A0: existing fixed40° orientation.
- A1: one deliberately specified bounded rotation profile through departure,
  ascent and receiving, returning to the canonical orientation at protected-body
  joins. Exact angles, knots and their geometric rationale require review first.

Keep the path, active-edge interval schedule, pressure and tool dimensions
identical between A0/A1. Otherwise the comparison cannot attribute the effect
to nib rotation. If rotation alone cannot meet thinness, report that incompatibility;
do not change edge fraction at the same time without a new explicit study.

Draw the actual oriented finite rectangle at a few labeled progress points,
including active corner and contact-center offset. Render the sweep through
the whole continuous orientation profile, not a centered variable-width line.
Update angle reflection and partial-contact frame tests before trusting pictures.

Rotation changes contact envelopes: recheck full finite-nib clearance and do not
reuse the fixed-orientation self-contact certificate without a new proof.
Respect the existing180°/s orientation-rate and3/s edge-endpoint-rate limits
when preparing physical timing. Normalized-progress preview is not proof that
those rates are feasible, and speed12mm/s is a cap, not a promised schedule.

## 6. What stays fixed; what needs explicit approval

No changes to tool width0.45mm, thickness0.02mm, nominal pressure0.5, thin-core
maximum `0.25*(0.45*cos40°+0.02*sin40°)` or minimum radius0.1125mm. Keep that
thinness reference fixed even when the nib rotates; do not rotate the threshold
along with the candidate. Do not average away peak width or curvature violations.

Source regions, internal ports, contact domains and possibly later spacing are
design choices to specify, not universal constants inherited from rejected
bridges. Broader contact or intentional retrace requires an explicit ownership/
permission decision. Changing topology cannot silently inherit old splice disks.
Keep aggregate length accounting meaningful across numerical subdivisions;
approve any changed evaluation partition before using it.

No fatigue, random variation, alphabet expansion, provider training or GPU/physical
execution in F1–F3. Nib behavior and path adaptation are studied separately first;
their later joint optimization is not authorized by this plan.
