## Observations

- Canonical n and u remain clearly recognizable. Their unconnected rendering is
  a useful reference, not a newly judged historical exemplar.
- The old bridge rises in a broad, visually continuous sweep. It looks smoother
  at the bottom, although it reads more like a span between the letters.
- The derived entry has a visibly flatter initial recovery followed by a more
  upright rise near the lower part. This is a real geometry difference, not
  copy/paste, scaling or a thickened line.
- That lower change produces a small shelf/shoulder before the long rise. To my
  eye, it feels less fluid than the old bridge there. This is a visual judgment,
  not evidence of a mathematical tangent discontinuity: the tangent checks pass.
- The upper approach remains similar, as expected with the fixed receiving span.
  T/S and the retained letter bodies are unchanged by construction and by drawing
  equality checks. Both candidates remain nominally thin through the connector.

## Assessment

The new construction makes the departure/recovery distinction more apparent,
but the images do not yet establish that it is more natural overall. I would
not promote it to a final preferred style solely because geometric/contact
tests pass. The shelf-to-rise transition is the clearest visual issue to discuss.

Next obtain owner feedback on that lower recovery and on whether the rise feels
like part of forming u. If the shelf is unwanted, investigate the semantic-port
placement and curvature progression there before proposing any further change.
Do not adjust the frozen candidate, refit, change limits or begin mu automatically.
